BEHIND THE SCENES

The Making Of Swarithmetic

Swarithmetic started with one slightly suspicious question: “What if you had to sing to solve maths?”

And somehow, it felt weird enough to build it.

കേട്ടപ്പോൾ അടിപൊളി ആയി തോന്നി അല്ലേ?
But implementation was the real task!!!

Our Experience — quick note: We laughed, we debugged, we sang into a stubborn microphone and bribed the UI with snacks. Below are short recordings/screenshots from the night — timestamped and slightly traumatized.

7:30 PM — “Njngal pani thudangi”

The idea arrived with grand flair and zero planning. We high-fived, named the mascot Luttappi, and immediately promised the internet this would be fun. (Famous last words.)

Humor note: If optimism were code, this commit would be 100% TODO.

8:30 PM – 10:30 PM — Food Mukhyam Bigilee

We paused for the sacred ritual: FOOD. Everything that follows was powered by snacks and the moral support of biryani.

There was singing. There was debugging between bites. There were crumbs in the keyboard. Silence = success.

Humor note: If CSS breaks, blame the curry.

10:30 PM – 11:30 PM — Making It Look Like A Game

We decided a math game should not look like a report. So we made neon, rounded corners, and an overcompensating mascot. Luttappi's wardrobe budget: one emoji.

Snapshot:
10:30–11:30 PM — Frontend snapshot

Humor note: Button hover animation = instant confidence boost.

11:30 PM – 1:00 AM — The Microphone Has Entered The Chat

We plugged in the microphone and the browser either listened or judged us. Pitch detection behaved like a moody roommate—sometimes helpful, often dramatic. We sang Sa Ri Ga while praying to the WebAudio gods.

Audio test:
11:30 PM–1:00 AM — Microphone test recording

Humor note: The microphone sometimes thought "Dha" was an aggressive cough.

1:00 AM – 2:00 AM — Teaching Maths To Music

We made numbers behave like notes. It was equal parts logic and karaoke. Variables learned to hum, operators discovered rhythm, and Luttappi danced. When equations finally cooperated we celebrated like we'd passed finals.

Welcome to the debugging marathon. We fixed one bug and accidentally created two more (classic). The game's behavior became sentient, demanding coffee and respect.

7:30 AM — The Morning After (Sort of)

We survived. There was triumphant confusion, some triumphant yawning, and a few "did we really just do that?" moments. The sun judged us silently.

THE FINAL PUSH

Sleep Was Around The Corner. We Ignored It.

“Change something. Refresh. Hope.”

There was actually no amount of sleep for us even though sleep was around the corner.

We just wanted to complete it.
And that was our only aim.

With the major features working, we shifted from pure development to making the project presentable.

We cleaned up the interface, checked the game flow, fixed obvious bugs and prepared the project for demonstration.

We also worked on the repository and documentation so that someone looking at Swarithmetic could understand what we had built without needing to sit beside us at 3 AM.

Humor note: Presentation mode is just the "make it look like you slept" mode.
